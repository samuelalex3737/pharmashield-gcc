# Q&A Preparation - PharmaShield GCC [GENAI]

Twenty-second answers for faculty and peer questions. Questions 1 to 9 are the master plan's defence matrix (section 6.4), rewritten where the 15 Nov overrides or the real results changed the answer. Questions 10 to 18 are new: they are the questions our own results invite. Owner = the member who answers first.

| # | Question | 20-second answer | Owner | What changed vs the plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why is your training data from 2006-2015? | SCMS teaches the model how pharmaceutical freight behaves: how plant history, freight profile, weight and mode drive delays. The suppliers we rank are real 2026 carboplatin makers from openFDA. We test on later years (2014-2015) than we train on, and in the pilot the model retrains monthly on the GPO's own ERP and Tatmeen data. | 4A | Feature list now matches override 2: origin parsed from the plant site; destination, insurance and PO lead time removed. |
| 2 | Doesn't low vendor overlap flatten your ML score? | Only 2 of the 11 makers appear in SCMS after parent mapping, and we say so. We do not use SCMS as a lookup: each maker is scored from its own plant country, lane and freight profile, with plant history where it exists. Scores range from 1.59% to 9.19%. Makers sharing a country and lacking plant history get the same score, because we refuse to invent differences we cannot observe. | 4A | Plan said "all 18 makers, differentiated scores"; the honest answer is 11 makers, 2 with history. |
| 3 | Why combine AHP-TOPSIS with ML instead of ML alone? | ML estimates risks; it cannot know how much the board cares about each risk for a life-critical drug. AHP captures that judgement, consistently (CR below 0.005). Entropy adds what the data says varies, and TOPSIS combines both into one auditable score. The MILP then turns the ranking into an exact, rule-compliant order. | 3 | Now explicitly a MILP, not an LP. |
| 4 | In a Hormuz crisis, who has time for an AHP survey? | Nobody, so the pairwise judgements are made once per mode, offline. In a crisis the buyer clicks Crisis and moves one dial, lambda, the price of risk. At lambda 1.5 the India-made share falls from 25% to 0% for 8.7% more cost, recomputed in the browser in milliseconds. | 3 | At the default lambda 1.0 the split does not change in Crisis mode; the answer now names lambda as the lever. |
| 5 | Won't a new supplier with no history always rank last? | No, and it will not rank first by accident either. Missing evidence is treated as neutral. New makers are probation candidates: they can win a 5-15% trial, at most 2 per order, if they pass a safety gate (no OAI inspection and no Class I recall in 3 years). 5 of 6 pass today. | 5 | Probation now means "not one of the 5 incumbents holding 98.3% of 2025 units" (A23), not "fewer than 5 SCMS shipments". |
| 6 | Isn't using US FDA data for a UAE GPO a stretch? | FDA inspections and recalls are a quality signal about a specific plant, wherever its product ships. That is why we match FDA records to each maker's own plants, not its whole group. It is US data, and we label it as such. In the pilot we would add the Emirates Drug Establishment registration status as the true local gate. | 2B | Dropped the claim that over 80% of GCC medicines come from FDA-inspected sites (override 11). |
| 7 | What if the model makes a wrong prediction? | It recommends, it never orders. Each score shows its top 3 reasons, a category manager approves or overrides with a written reason, and every decision is logged. We report false alarms openly: XGBoost raises 784 against 1,590 for the baseline, and false-alarm rate is a pilot KPI. | 5 | Added the measured false-alarm numbers. |
| 8 | How do you handle UAE data privacy and geographic fairness? | The audit log stores business decisions, not personal data, in line with the UAE PDPL (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021). On fairness, we use plant- and lane-level evidence where available and country-level evidence otherwise, and we show which one each score rests on. | 5 | Wording changed from "never by country" (override 10). |
| 9 | What is the ROI of the 90-day pilot? | Resilience has a price, and we state it: the risk-balanced split costs 17.3% more than single-sourcing the cheapest maker, which our Tier 1 rules forbid. In a crisis, 8.7% more removes the exposed lane. The pilot measures dual-source coverage, a pre-qualified bench, on-time-in-full delivery and the false-alarm rate. The goal is to reduce stock-outs. | 1 | "Eliminate stock-outs" became "reduce" (override 11); costs now come from the MILP. |
| 10 | Your top-ranked maker, AVYXA, has almost no evidence. Isn't that a red flag? | Yes, and the system handles it. AVYXA scores well on what we can see: a US plant and no recalls. Its compliance terms are neutral because it has no inspection on file. That is exactly why it is a probation candidate: it gets 5%, not 70%. The primary, Teva, is the best-ranked established maker. | 3 | New. |
| 11 | Why does Crisis mode not change the split at lambda 1.0? | Under our route assumptions, the exposure gap between India-made and US-made supply is small (C5 0.32 against 0.24 in Crisis). The ranking shifts, but price still dominates at lambda 1.0. The sensitivity table shows the board exactly where the split moves (lambda 1.5) and what it costs. Hiding that behind a forced reshuffle would be dishonest. | 3 | New. |
| 12 | Why Medicaid prices for a GCC buyer? | There are no public GCC tender prices by maker. Medicaid 2025 gives real per-unit prices for 6 of the 11 makers, which is enough to see relative price levels. The other 5 are imputed at the median and flagged "C1 imputed" in the app, and they do not influence the entropy weights. | 2A | New (A4). |
| 13 | An NLP F1 of 0.987 looks too good. Is it? | Yes, if read naively. FDA writes citations from standard templates, so the text is very predictable. That is why we hand-checked: the labels match a human reading about 83% of the time. We present the tags as auditor pointers, not verdicts. | 4B | New. |
| 14 | How did you choose 5% as the incumbent threshold? | From the data: the 5 incumbents hold 98.3% of 2025 Medicaid units. The smallest holds 9.8% and the largest non-incumbent 1.7%, so any threshold above 1.8% and up to 9.8% gives the same split. | 2B | New (A23). |
| 15 | Are demand and capacity real? | No. They are scenario assumptions (10,000 vials a month, A11; capacities, A27 and A28), labelled on the slide. The MILP structure is what matters: replace them with the GPO's real figures and the same model runs. | 3 | New. |
| 16 | Why remove the WGI political-stability index? | It would add a second country-level penalty with no lane evidence behind it, which conflicts with our fairness rule. Route exposure uses the logistics index (LPI) and the lane mix only. | 2B | New (decision D7, override 12). |
| 17 | How do you know the browser and Python give the same answer? | The TypeScript engine is tested against the Python outputs: 14 parity tests cover weights (within 1e-9), TOPSIS scores (within 1e-6) and the MILP split (within 1 vial, including every lambda in the sensitivity table). The app also runs a parity check at load; if it fails, it shows the precomputed Python results. | 5 | New. |
| 18 | Why only one drug? | The unit of analysis is maker x SKU, and the same pipeline runs per SKU. We chose carboplatin because it is Tier 1, currently short, and has 11 qualified makers. The pilot extends it to 5 Tier 1 oncology SKUs. | 1 | New. |
